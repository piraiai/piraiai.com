How It Works

LimeSurvey imports surveys via LSS files - XML-based survey structure files generated by LimeSurvey's own export or by external tools. Pirai AI reads your Word, PDF, or Excel document and produces an LSS file ready for direct import into your LimeSurvey instance. Supports both LSS DB version 5 (LimeSurvey 3.x) and version 6 (LimeSurvey 4.x and 5.x). Select the correct version for your installation during export.

LSS Version 5 vs Version 6

LimeSurvey's LSS format has two major database versions that are not interchangeable. Importing a version 6 LSS into a LimeSurvey 3.x installation (which uses DB version 5) will fail, and vice versa.

LSS DB VersionCompatible LimeSurvey Version
DB Version 5LimeSurvey 3.x
DB Version 6LimeSurvey 4.x and 5.x (most current installations)

When downloading from Pirai AI, select the LSS version that matches your LimeSurvey installation. If you are unsure, check your LimeSurvey version in Admin > Global Settings > Overview. Most current installations use version 5.x and need DB version 6.

How to Import an LSS File into LimeSurvey

1

Upload your document and download LSS

Go to platform.piraiai.com. Upload your Word, PDF, or Excel file. Select LimeSurvey as the target. Select the correct DB version (5 or 6). Download the .lss file.

2

Log into LimeSurvey Admin

Go to your LimeSurvey installation's admin panel. Navigate to Survey List in the top menu.

3

Import the LSS file

Click Import Survey. Select your .lss file. Click Import. The survey structure - all questions, question types, groups, and answer options - is imported instantly.

4

Review and configure

Open the imported survey in the LimeSurvey editor. Review question types, add conditions (branching logic), configure tokens if needed, and activate the survey for data collection.

Why Teams Use LimeSurvey

Open source and self-hosted. LimeSurvey Community Edition is free and open source. Self-hosting keeps all survey data on your own servers - important for institutions with data residency requirements, government organisations, and academic institutions in jurisdictions with strict data governance.

Strong branching and conditions. LimeSurvey's conditions and equation logic are more powerful than Google Forms and comparable to mid-tier commercial platforms. Complex skip logic, expression-based conditions, and token-based personalisation are all supported natively.

Multilingual surveys. LimeSurvey supports surveys in multiple languages with full right-to-left text support. For international research and government survey programmes requiring multilingual deployment, LimeSurvey's built-in translation workflow is a significant advantage.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is an LSS file?+

An LSS file is LimeSurvey's native survey export format - an XML file containing the complete structure of a survey including question groups, questions, answer options, conditions, and settings. It can be imported into any LimeSurvey installation of the compatible database version. See the full LSS file guide at /blog/what-is-an-lss-file.

How do I convert a Word document to LimeSurvey?+

Upload your Word document to Pirai AI at platform.piraiai.com, select LimeSurvey as the target platform, choose the correct LSS DB version for your installation (version 5 for LimeSurvey 3.x, version 6 for 4.x and 5.x), and download the .lss file. In LimeSurvey admin, go to Survey List > Import Survey, upload the file, and click Import.

What is the difference between LSS DB version 5 and 6?+

LSS DB version 5 is compatible with LimeSurvey 3.x installations. LSS DB version 6 is compatible with LimeSurvey 4.x and 5.x. The two versions are not interchangeable - importing the wrong version into your installation will fail. Check your LimeSurvey version in Admin > Global Settings before downloading from Pirai AI.

Is LimeSurvey free?+

LimeSurvey Community Edition is free and open source. You can self-host it on your own servers at no software cost. LimeSurvey also offers a hosted cloud service with paid plans if you prefer not to manage your own server. For academic institutions and government organisations requiring data residency on their own infrastructure, the free self-hosted option is the most common choice.
