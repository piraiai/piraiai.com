Word to Google Forms - One Click

Upload your Word document. Click Convert to Google Forms. Authorise with your Google account. Your Google Form is created in your Drive instantly. No downloading scripts. No opening a Script Editor. No pasting code. One click from your Word document to a live Google Form.

How to Convert Word to Google Forms

1. Upload your Word file at platform.piraiai.com. Pirai AI reads your document and extracts every question - numbered lists, bulleted options, headings, tables, and open-ended prompts - and identifies the correct question type for each.

2. Review your questions in the Pirai AI workspace. See every question with its detected type and answer options before anything is created. Change question types, edit wording, add or remove options. What you see here is exactly what appears in your Google Form.

3. Click Convert to Google Forms. One click. A Google sign-in window opens.

4. Authorise with Google. Sign in with the Google account where you want the form created. Click Allow. This grants Pirai AI one-time permission to create a form in your Drive. You can revoke this at any time.

5. Open your new Google Form. Pirai AI creates the form directly in your Google Drive. Every question from your Word document is in the form, with the correct question type, in the correct order, with all answer options in place. Click the link to open it.

What Transfers From Word to Google Forms

  • Multiple choice questions - each option becomes a Google Forms Multiple Choice option
  • Select all that apply questions - become Checkboxes questions with all options
  • Open-ended questions - become Short Answer or Paragraph depending on expected response length
  • Rating scales (1-5, 1-7, 1-10) - become Linear Scale questions with start and end labels
  • Matrix / grid questions - become Multiple Choice Grid questions with row items and column options
  • Section headings - become sections in the Google Form separating question groups

Word Document Structures That Convert Well

Pirai AI reads the structure of your Word document, not just the text. It detects questions from numbered lists, bulleted answer options, heading-separated sections, and tables. The most common formats it handles:

  • Q1 / Q2 / Q3 format with lettered or bulleted answer options below each question
  • Word headings (Heading 1, Heading 2) as section dividers
  • Word tables with row items and column scale options for matrix questions
  • Instruction text ("Select all that apply", "Please describe") for question type detection
