Qualtrics GDPR and EU Data Residency: Guide for European Universities

Arun Raj•August 05, 2026•5 min read•Guide
Qualtrics in European Research

Qualtrics is used at European universities for academic research surveys, student experience measurement, and institutional research. GDPR compliance requires that data transfers to the US have an adequate legal basis. Qualtrics offers EU data centre options (Frankfurt and Dublin) and Data Processing Agreements under GDPR Article 28. Understanding your institution's Qualtrics data residency configuration is essential before collecting personal data from EU participants.

Qualtrics EU Data Centres

Qualtrics operates data centres in Frankfurt (AWS EU-Central-1) and Dublin (AWS EU-West-1). When EU data residency is configured in your institutional licence, survey responses are stored in the specified EU data centre rather than US infrastructure. This resolves the primary GDPR international transfer concern - data does not leave the EU/EEA.

To confirm whether your institution's Qualtrics licence includes EU data residency: check your contract, contact your IT services team, or ask your Qualtrics account manager. If EU data residency is not currently configured, it can typically be added at contract renewal or by request - the technical configuration change is handled by Qualtrics.

Data Processing Agreement Requirements

GDPR Article 28 requires a Data Processing Agreement (DPA) between the data controller (your institution) and any data processor (Qualtrics). Qualtrics provides a standard DPA as part of institutional licence agreements. Key elements your institution's DPA with Qualtrics should cover:

  • Data processing purposes and scope
  • Data residency configuration (EU data centre)
  • Sub-processor list and notification requirements
  • Data deletion timelines after contract termination
  • Security measures and breach notification procedures
  • Data subject rights assistance

If your institution does not have a current DPA with Qualtrics, contact your Data Protection Officer - operating without a DPA is a GDPR compliance gap for any processing of personal data.

Schrems II and Standard Contractual Clauses

The 2020 Schrems II decision (C-311/18) invalidated the EU-US Privacy Shield and imposed additional requirements on EU-to-US data transfers. For Qualtrics without EU data residency, data transfers to the US require Standard Contractual Clauses (SCCs) plus a Transfer Impact Assessment (TIA) demonstrating that SCCs provide effective protection in the specific transfer context.

With EU data residency configured, there is no EU-to-US transfer - Schrems II does not apply. This is the cleaner GDPR compliance path and why EU data residency configuration matters for European Qualtrics users.

When to Use LimeSurvey or REDCap Instead

Even with EU data residency configured, Qualtrics remains a third-party cloud processor. For research involving GDPR Article 9 special category data (health, biometric, genetic data, ethnicity, political opinions), your DPO may require self-hosted infrastructure. In these cases, self-hosted LimeSurvey or REDCap on institutional EU servers is typically the preferred option - no third-party processor is involved and institutional data governance applies directly.

Frequently Asked Questions

Does Qualtrics store data in Europe?+

Qualtrics offers EU data centre options in Frankfurt (Germany) and Dublin (Ireland). Whether your institution's data is stored in these EU data centres depends on your licence configuration. Contact your institution's IT services or Qualtrics account manager to confirm your data residency configuration.

Is Qualtrics GDPR compliant for European research?+

Qualtrics can be configured for GDPR compliance: EU data residency (Frankfurt or Dublin), Data Processing Agreement under Article 28, and Standard Contractual Clauses for any residual US transfers. Compliance depends on your institutional licence configuration and the presence of a valid DPA. Contact your institution's Data Protection Officer to verify.

What is the Schrems II impact on Qualtrics in Europe?+

The Schrems II decision requires that EU-to-US data transfers have SCCs plus a Transfer Impact Assessment. Qualtrics with EU data residency avoids this issue entirely - data does not leave the EU. Without EU data residency, your institution needs SCCs and a TIA for the Qualtrics data transfer, which should be arranged through your DPO and Qualtrics account team.

Should European researchers use Qualtrics or LimeSurvey for GDPR compliance?+

For most European university research, Qualtrics with EU data residency and a valid DPA is GDPR compliant. For research involving GDPR special category data where your DPO requires self-hosted infrastructure, LimeSurvey or REDCap on institutional EU servers provides stronger compliance by avoiding any third-party cloud processor.
