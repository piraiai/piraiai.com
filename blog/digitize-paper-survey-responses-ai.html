How to Digitize Paper Survey Responses with AI (Qualtrics, SurveyMonkey & More)

A stack of scanned paper survey responses does not turn itself into a spreadsheet. Someone has to open each one, read the marks and handwriting, and type the answers in, question by question, form by form. It is slow, it is mind-numbing, and every hour of it introduces a fresh chance for a transcription error. Why Manual Entry (and Closed Paper-Survey Platforms) Fall Short

Manual data entry does not scale. Retyping a few dozen responses is tedious but survivable; retyping a few thousand is a project of its own, and every question type, matrix grids, rank orders, open text, needs its own workaround from whoever is typing.

Dedicated paper-survey platforms solve the scale problem but introduce a different one: most of them only work if you design and print your survey inside their own system, often with a proprietary layout or a QR code on every page. If your survey already exists somewhere else, Qualtrics, SurveyMonkey, or any of the other platforms teams actually use, that means rebuilding it from scratch just to get the paper responses back as data.

Lens takes a different starting point: it assumes your survey already exists. You bring the questions you already have, and it reads the paper against them.

Optical Mark Recognition vs. AI-Based Digitization

Most paper-survey tools that have been around for a while are built on Optical Mark Recognition (OMR): a scanner detects marks by their exact position on a pre-aligned, machine-readable template. It is fast and reliable, provided the form was printed in that specific layout to begin with.

Lens works differently. It reads the page the way a person would, then checks what it finds against your survey's own known questions and options, rather than a fixed grid of expected mark positions. That means it can work from a printed Qualtrics survey, a SurveyMonkey export, or any other layout, without the form needing to be built for a scanner in the first place. It also means it can read something OMR alone cannot: handwritten short answers and essay responses, transcribed against your survey's own answer key rather than guessed freely.

Checked against your survey, not guessed

Every answer Lens extracts is verified against your survey's own known questions, options, and items. If something on the page cannot be matched with confidence, it is flagged for review, not silently recorded.
