Qualtrics vs REDCap: Which Platform for Your Research Survey? (2026)

Arun Raj•July 18, 2026•3 min read•Glossary
Definition

Qualtrics and REDCap serve different primary purposes despite surface-level overlap. REDCap is purpose-built for regulated clinical data capture with audit trails, field validation, and institutional sovereignty. Qualtrics is a general-purpose survey and experience management platform with superior reporting, CX integration, and non-regulated research capabilities.

TL;DR

Use REDCap for regulated clinical trials, studies requiring ICH GCP audit trails, 21 CFR Part 11 electronic records compliance, and any research where the data must live on institution-controlled servers. REDCap is the standard for academic medical centre clinical data capture globally.

Use Qualtrics for patient satisfaction programmes (HCAHPS, CG-CAHPS), staff wellbeing surveys, patient-reported outcome programmes outside regulated trial infrastructure, and general academic research where dashboard reporting and EHR integration are priorities.

Use both together for institutions with academic and operational survey needs - REDCap for research, Qualtrics for patient experience and staff surveys. Pirai AI converts instrument documents to either platform, so the structural build time is minimal regardless of choice.

When REDCap Is the Right Choice

Regulated clinical trials. ICH GCP requires electronic data capture systems to have audit trails, user access logs, and documented validation. REDCap with institutional validation documentation (IQ/OQ/PQ) satisfies these requirements in academic medical centre contexts. Qualtrics is not designed for this use case.

Institution-controlled data sovereignty. Some institutions, particularly those working with patient data in jurisdictions with strict data residency requirements, need survey data to live on institution-owned or institution-controlled servers. REDCap's on-premises or institutional cloud deployment provides this; Qualtrics does not unless the FedRAMP or Health Cloud editions are specifically procured.

Composite score calculation in the platform. REDCap's calculated fields automatically compute PHQ-9 totals, GAD-7 totals, BMI, and custom scoring formulas as respondents complete the survey. These calculated values can trigger branching logic and alerts. Qualtrics' Scoring feature provides similar capability but with less flexibility for complex multi-field formulas.

Longitudinal and multi-instrument studies. REDCap's Events and Instruments architecture is designed for studies with multiple assessment timepoints and multiple data collection instruments per participant. The same participant record links across instruments and timepoints natively. Qualtrics manages this through separate surveys and data linking in external tools.

When Qualtrics Is the Right Choice

Patient satisfaction programmes. HCAHPS, CG-CAHPS, and other CMS-mandated patient experience surveys are operational rather than regulated research programmes. Qualtrics' CX dashboard, automated distribution triggered by EHR discharge events, and real-time reporting are better suited to this use case than REDCap's research-oriented data structure.

Staff and employee surveys. Qualtrics EmployeeXM is purpose-built for engagement, wellbeing, and 360 surveys. REDCap is not designed for HR survey use cases. Clinical staff wellbeing surveys, leadership effectiveness surveys, and culture assessments all belong in Qualtrics.

Large-scale social science research. For psychology, education, and social science research where the dataset is large, the question types are standard (matrix, MCQ, text entry), and the analysis is done in SPSS or R, Qualtrics' panel integration, randomisation features, and SPSS export are well-suited. REDCap's data structure (designed for individual patient records) is less natural for large cross-sectional survey datasets.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is REDCap better than Qualtrics for clinical research?+

REDCap is better for regulated clinical trials requiring ICH GCP audit trails, 21 CFR Part 11 compliance, and institution-controlled data sovereignty. Qualtrics is better for patient satisfaction programmes, staff surveys, and non-regulated research where real-time dashboards and EHR integration are priorities. Most academic medical centres use both.
