ARC and NHMRC Research Survey Data Collection: Platform Guide for 2026

Arun Raj•August 04, 2026•5 min read•Guide
ARC and NHMRC Research Context

ARC (Australian Research Council) and NHMRC (National Health and Medical Research Council) are the two largest competitive research funding bodies in Australia. Together they fund the majority of university-based research that involves survey data collection. The funding body, the nature of the research, and the HREC requirements together determine which survey platform is appropriate - not the researcher's personal preference.

ARC-Funded Research and Survey Platforms

ARC funds research across natural sciences, engineering, social sciences, and humanities through Discovery Projects, Linkage Projects, DECRA (Discovery Early Career Researcher Award), Future Fellowships, and ARC Laureate Fellowships. Survey data collection in ARC-funded research is most commonly conducted via:

Qualtrics - the standard for ARC social science, psychology, education, and population studies. Available via institutional licence at most universities. Supports complex survey logic, block randomisation, SPSS/CSV export, and online panels via Qualtrics Audience. Appropriate for studies that do not involve sensitive health data or require institutional data sovereignty.

Google Forms - for low-risk, anonymous surveys in ARC-funded research where institutional Qualtrics access is not required by the HREC and the research does not involve sensitive information.

ARC does not specify a mandated survey platform in grant agreements. The platform choice is determined by the researcher, HREC requirements, and institutional availability.

NHMRC-Funded Research and Survey Platforms

NHMRC funds biomedical and clinical health research through Ideas Grants, Investigator Grants, Synergy Grants, Partnership Projects, and Centre of Research Excellence schemes. Survey data collection in NHMRC-funded research is most commonly conducted via:

REDCap - the standard for NHMRC-funded clinical trials, observational studies involving health data, and patient-reported outcome research. REDCap on institutional servers keeps health data in Australia, satisfies ICH GCP requirements, and provides the audit trail required for regulated research. Some NHMRC grant agreements or sponsor agreements for clinical trials specifically require REDCap.

Qualtrics - used for NHMRC-funded population health surveys, public health questionnaires, and patient experience research that does not involve sensitive clinical data or require GCP-level audit trails.

ARC and NHMRC AI Guidance (April 2026)

In April 2026, ARC and NHMRC jointly released updated AI guidance applying to grant applications and peer review. The key points relevant to using AI tools in research data collection:

  • AI tools may be used as support tools when preparing grant applications and research materials
  • Responsibility for accuracy and integrity remains with the researcher
  • Safeguards must protect confidentiality, privacy, and research integrity
  • Researchers must not input confidential peer review information into AI tools

Using Pirai AI to convert a research instrument Word document into a Qualtrics QSF or REDCap Data Dictionary is consistent with this guidance. The instrument document itself is not confidential peer review information - it is research methodology that the researcher has designed. The researcher retains responsibility for verifying that the converted instrument accurately matches the approved HREC protocol wording.

Data Sovereignty Under Australian Privacy Principles

Australian Privacy Principle 8 (APP 8) requires organisations to take reasonable steps to ensure that overseas recipients of personal information handle it consistently with the APPs. For research data:

  • REDCap on institutional servers - no cross-border transfer; satisfies APP 8 without additional measures
  • Qualtrics (US cloud) - cross-border transfer; covered by institutional data processing agreements with Qualtrics; acceptable for most HREC protocols with appropriate disclosure in the participant information statement
  • Google Forms (personal account) - cross-border transfer; limited data processing protections; may require additional HREC justification for research involving sensitive information

Frequently Asked Questions

What survey platform should I use for NHMRC-funded research?+

NHMRC clinical research typically uses REDCap on institutional servers, which provides GCP-aligned audit trails and keeps health data in Australia without cross-border transfer. For NHMRC population health and patient experience research without clinical data requirements, Qualtrics via institutional licence is commonly used. Check your grant agreement - some NHMRC-funded clinical trial agreements specifically require REDCap.

What survey platform should I use for ARC-funded research?+

ARC does not mandate a specific survey platform. Qualtrics (via institutional licence) is the most common choice for ARC social science, psychology, education, and population studies, given its complex logic support and SPSS export. Google Forms is appropriate for low-risk anonymous surveys. The choice depends on your HREC requirements and the sensitivity of data being collected.

Does the ARC/NHMRC AI guidance (April 2026) affect using AI tools for survey setup?+

The April 2026 ARC and NHMRC joint AI guidance allows AI as a support tool for research activities, with researchers remaining responsible for accuracy and integrity. Using Pirai AI to convert a research instrument document to Qualtrics or REDCap format is a support tool use consistent with this guidance - the researcher must verify that the converted instrument matches the HREC-approved protocol.

Do I need to disclose data storage arrangements to my HREC?+

Yes. Australian HREC applications require you to describe where data will be stored and what data security arrangements are in place. For Qualtrics (US cloud), describe the institutional data processing agreement. For REDCap on institutional servers, describe the institutional data governance framework. For Google Forms, note that data is stored on US-based servers.
