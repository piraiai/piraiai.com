Best REDCap Converter for US Clinical Research: Word and PDF to Data Dictionary

Arun Raj•August 21, 2026•5 min read•Guide
REDCap Conversion for US Clinical Research

REDCap is deployed at more than 6,000 institutions worldwide through the Vanderbilt-managed REDCap Consortium, and is the standard EDC for NIH-funded US clinical trials. Building a REDCap instrument from a sponsor-provided CRF or validated scale PDF requires creating a Data Dictionary CSV - an 18-column structured file that REDCap uses to build every field. Manual Data Dictionary creation takes 4-8 hours for a 40-item CRF. Pirai AI generates the Data Dictionary CSV from any Word or PDF instrument document automatically.

REDCap at US Universities and Medical Centers

REDCap is confirmed at virtually every US medical school and academic health system. Major consortium members include Vanderbilt, Mayo Clinic, Johns Hopkins, Mass General Brigham, University of California system, University of Michigan, Duke, Northwestern, Emory, and hundreds more. Access is typically through your institution's research informatics team, CTSA (Clinical and Translational Science Awards) programme, or IT department.

NIH's National Center for Advancing Translational Sciences (NCATS) supports REDCap deployment through CTSA-funded institutions. If your institution has a CTSA, your REDCap access request routes through the CTSA informatics core.

HIPAA and REDCap for US Health Research

REDCap on US institutional servers satisfies HIPAA requirements for electronic protected health information (ePHI) in research. The institution's HIPAA Security Rule compliance framework covers the REDCap instance - including access controls, audit logs, and encryption. No Business Associate Agreement with a cloud vendor is required because data does not leave institutional servers. For NIH-funded clinical trials requiring HIPAA compliance, institutional REDCap is the most straightforward path.

Converting US Clinical Instruments to REDCap

Common US clinical research instruments converted to REDCap Data Dictionary using Pirai AI:

  • PROMIS measures - NIH-developed patient-reported outcomes used across CTSA-affiliated studies
  • PHQ-9 and GAD-7 - standard US mental health screening in primary care research
  • SF-36 and SF-12 - widely used in NIH-funded health outcomes research
  • PCL-5 (PTSD Checklist) - VA and DoD-funded PTSD research
  • AUDIT-C - alcohol use screening in US preventive health research
  • Sponsor-provided CRFs - FDA-regulated IND studies with sponsor-specified data collection forms
  • Custom study instruments - IRB-approved questionnaires drafted in Word by the study team
